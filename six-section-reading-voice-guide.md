# Six-Section Reading Voice Guide

Status: approved editorial standard for the six-section Vedic reading's customer prose.
Locked through Jason's Sep 2026 revision rounds on three sample charts (Austin/1988,
Rochester/1978, Westover PA/1981 — the last approved without a further correction round,
cross-checked and confirmed by a second reviewer). Governs voice and structure; does not by
itself authorize any interpretive content — Section 2 content still runs through the existing
adoption-provenance process, and Sections 3–5 content still runs through the interpretation
workbook's practitioner review before anything here becomes development- or production-eligible.

Supersedes the old `editorial/gold-standard-complete-reading.md` fictional example for VOICE
purposes only. That file is left untouched and still governs the locked ~245–290 word-per-section
/ ~1,500–1,600 total contract, which this guide does not change.

## Structure

One "Before We Begin" intro before the six locked sections. "Explain the concept, then apply it"
belongs only where it naturally fits — not at the top of every section. Repeating a definition in
each section makes the reading feel like a textbook.

**Before We Begin (intro):**
- Explain what a Moon sign represents, and that nakshatras are a real interpretive layer used for
  every planet, not just the Moon — keep this general, no per-planet enumeration
  ("your Sun has one, your Venus has one...").
- State this reading focuses mainly on the Moon's nakshatra, because the Moon shapes emotional
  life most directly.
- State the person's Moon sign and Moon nakshatra by name, and their combined plain-language
  meaning.
- Name the nakshatra exactly once in the whole reading, here. After that, apply its influence
  without repeating the name. Zero uses of "pada" anywhere. No other nakshatra named anywhere.
- Do NOT put the Rahu/Ketu explanation here — it belongs at the start of Section 5.

**Section 1 (guiding planet):** may briefly explain the rising sign and guiding planet in 1–2
natural sentences woven into the section, not a separate lesson.

**Section 1 opening line (personalization):** the first sentence of Section 1 must be:

    [First name], your Vedic chart begins with [Rising Sign] rising.

This replaces the previous unnamed opening sentence — it is not added above it. Continue directly
into the chart-specific explanation on the next sentence, exactly as before (no separate lesson,
no extra transition). Example (Matthew, Gemini rising):

> Matthew, your Vedic chart begins with Gemini rising. This is your starting instinct: noticing
> more than one angle on a situation, and staying curious enough that a first impression rarely
> stays your final one.

Name rules:
- Use the person's first or preferred name only, never their full name, in reading prose. A full
  name may appear in a chart/reading header, never as conversational address in the body.
- Use the name exactly once, in that first sentence of Section 1. Do not repeat it elsewhere in
  Section 1, and do not force it into any later section or the takeaway.
- No bold, italics, enlargement, or placing the name on its own line — plain inline prose only.
- Preserve the name's legitimate capitalization, hyphens, and apostrophes exactly as given.
- If no usable first or preferred name is supplied, fall back to the original unnamed opening:
  `Your Vedic chart begins with [Rising Sign] rising.`
- The name changes only the opening address. It must never affect chart interpretation, claim
  selection, or personalization anywhere else in the reading.

**Section 2 (Moon/mind):** move directly into lived meaning. Do not reopen with a placement
statement ("Your Moon sits in your third house...") or restate the sign/nakshatra — the intro
already established those facts. Should read as a continuation, not a second introduction.

**Section 3 (relationships):** discuss directly, no 7th-house/Venus mechanics lecture. Balanced
framing only: fairness and equal participation, shared values/money/family responsibilities/
promises/communication as central, wanting problems addressed directly, directness that can get
too sharp under pressure, honesty without turning disagreement into something that must be won,
independence and closeness both needing room, practical commitment mattering as much as
attraction. Do not portray the person as selfish, controlling, manipulative, or emotionally
careless, and do not moralize, unless a reviewed claim explicitly supports that language.

**Section 4 (dharma/work):** discuss purpose, abilities, working conditions, and specific fields
directly, no dharma/10th-house lecture. Must explicitly include: what kind of contribution feels
purposeful, the person's strongest useful abilities, the working conditions those abilities need,
specific reasonable fields (never framed as guaranteed careers), the main work-related challenge,
and a practical direction for handling it.

**Section 5 (Rahu/Ketu):** the explanation of what the nodes are belongs at the START of this
section, not the intro: not physical bodies, the two points where the Moon's orbit crosses the
Sun's path (the eclipse alignment); Rahu = north node, Ketu = south node. In Vedic tradition Ketu
points to natural gifts/abilities already carried into this life (familiar, sometimes called
past-life strengths, available without much effort); Rahu points to what's being actively grown
into this lifetime (less familiar, more effort, real potential). May include one general sentence
on the push-pull dynamic between the familiar (Ketu) side and the growth (Rahu) side, wherever
that axis falls in the chart. After that, apply the specific placements without re-teaching node
mechanics. Do not assert a universal "growth versus release" psychology as settled fact. Avoid
vague predictive phrases ("success may come") and vague filler ("a second, serious influence") —
describe what any additional planetary influence actually does in plain terms.

**Section 6 (takeaway):** pure synthesis of threads already introduced in 1–5. No new chart factor
or technical explanation. Should weave together whatever threads *this* reading actually
established (do not force a fixed checklist from a different chart) — not merely repeat sentences
from earlier sections.

## Voice and reading level

- Sounds like a person speaking, not a technical document or astrology lecture.
- Establish the interpretive frame once (e.g. "Your chart suggests"), then write with natural
  confidence — do not hedge every sentence with "may." Possibility language should remain present
  but not make every sentence sound uncertain.
- Roughly 7th–8th grade reading level: familiar words, mostly short sentences (split long
  run-ons — target average sentence length well under 20 words), concrete human situations, no
  academic psychological language, no vague filler phrases ("a quieter thread underneath," "your
  pull toward flexibility"), no sentence included merely to reach a word count.
- No 6+ word phrase may repeat word-for-word across two sections of the same reading.
- Target length ~1,500–1,600 words including the intro — a target, not a padding requirement. If
  genuine required content pushes the total higher (e.g. properly explaining what the nodes are),
  let it grow rather than cut real content; report honestly if a section runs short instead of
  padding it.
- Every claim must trace to already-established chart facts, the interpretation workbook, or
  existing pipeline claims — no new unsupported interpretations.

## Delivery process

Draft the complete six-section reading in plain Markdown and share it directly for review BEFORE
any DOCX/PDF/packaging or pipeline changes. Once approved, package as a practitioner-review
document (see `editorial/six-section-samples/`), never as a claim to the active pipeline without
going through the same adoption-review process Section 2 uses.
